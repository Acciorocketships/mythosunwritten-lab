7. ONE RESIDUAL, FOUND HERE AND NOT PREVIOUSLY RECORDED
-------------------------------------------------------
Thirty-four of the sixty-five suites boot a second engine with OS.execute, at 40
call sites in all (test_goals:609, test_checks:656, test_turn_seam:226,
test_grass four times, and so on). Those children are SceneTree scripts
of exactly the shape the old runner had: all 54 entry points under bin/ extend
SceneTree, do their work in _initialize() and call quit() at the end of it. A runtime error in one of them therefore abandons _initialize() before the
quit(), and the child never exits. Demonstrated on a scratch child of that shape
rather than inferred from the code:

    $ cat tools/critic_child.gd        # extends SceneTree; _initialize() reads rows[1] then quit(0)
    $ timeout 30 godot4 --headless --path . --script res://tools/critic_child.gd
    child EXIT=124  elapsed=30s
    child: started
    SCRIPT ERROR: Out of bounds get index '1' (on base: 'Array[Dictionary]')
    (`child: finished` never printed; the process was still alive at 30s)

What that costs a real run: the parent suite blocks inside OS.execute, the run
prints nothing, and the wrapper's silence watchdog ends it -- after
RUN_TESTS_SILENCE, which defaults to 7200 seconds. So it is bounded and reported
and is never a false pass, which is the property this milestone was after. But
the "waited on forever" half of the original complaint still exists one level
down, at two hours a time. The suites already assert `equal(int(code), 0)` on
their children, so launching them under `timeout` would turn those two hours into
a named failed check in seconds. Nothing here is broken; this is a cheap
improvement, recorded so it is not re-discovered by waiting for it.


8. THE TWO DISCLOSURES, JUDGED
-------------------------------
(a) The bargain draw that ships was the second one run (I-fb01a6a5b407).

Confirmed by reading tests/test_bargain.gd. Six of its checks live in one method,
_the_model_driven_trader_answered_and_the_item_arrived (lines 194-217): the
trader accepted a bargain, the lantern is in the person's pack, the person paid
the price, the trader no longer carries it, the sale closed what the trader was
after, and the pack is shut again. Every one of those is a claim about what one
language-model draw happened to do. The other methods -- the pack was shown, the
ask was built from the keyboard, the trader learned exactly as much, a proposal
was denied in the engine's own words, two replays print the same bytes -- are
claims about machinery and hold whatever the model says.

So the suite's colour does turn on which draw was kept, for six of its checks,
and the kept draw was kept because those checks passed. That is selection on the
outcome of the test and it should be said plainly. Two things keep it from
undermining the milestone. It is disclosed, twice and in the sharpest available
words -- reports/observation-position.md's section "The bargain table was
recorded twice, and here is why", and reports/dialogue-trade.md:125-134, which
says outright that "re-recording this one table is a lottery with the suite's
colour as the prize". And the disclosure is falsifiable and was tested by its
author: with the same coarsened packet and the old 2026-09-07 rows replayed by
position, the sale closes and the suite passes, so the packet is not what stopped
it. A green run is therefore believable for what this suite demonstrates about
the machinery, and the six end-to-end checks should be read as a statement about
one draw until they are split.

(b) The collapse the re-record was scheduled to repair had already been repaired
four commits earlier (I-ade9ce281b4a).

The question routed here was whether a recorded-replies count that can be
repaired as a side effect of an unrelated re-record is a check that means
anything. It is, but only one thing: it is a drift detector, not a quality
measure. It answers "does the checked-in recording still answer the questions
this run puts", and any live re-record restores it to all-of-them by
construction. That is why 09764d8 repaired it as a side effect: 09764d8 added
three rows to the action catalogue and re-recorded live for its own reason. That
is the detector working, not a fluke. What it cannot do is fail after a
re-record, so it can never be evidence that a new draw is a good draw -- which is
exactly the gap the bargain suite falls into.

The consequence for the item is real but narrow: W-observation-rerecord's
scheduling premise ("the one failing check in the whole suite today") was four
commits stale, and the milestone description that repeated it was wrong. The work
itself stands: the position line was settled on its own merits, and the
measurement the item was asked for came back negative and was reported as
negative -- no representation of a position recovers any of the 56 questions the
walk-motion change cost, so the class of change that still invalidates a
recording is any change to how far a character has got when it is asked. A
negative result reported as negative is the opposite of the failure this
milestone was about.
