2. what the die costs on average
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  The swing is centred and the second step rounds to nearest, so a
  blow's mean is the deterministic number itself. Folding the swing in
  beside the multiplier and flooring once gave ratios of 0.85 to 0.99
  instead -- a quiet nerf on every attack in the game, which is what
  this table was written to catch.

3. the positional ladder, at every roll
----------------------------------------------------------------------

  manoeuvred round the back would sometimes be punished for it. The
  worst roll on a rung is compared with the best roll on the one below.

5. what the two options that were not chosen would have cost
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Arithmetic, not simulation: a to-hit roll makes a planned sequence
  of n blows land with probability (1-p)^n, for a single-blow miss
  chance p. Nothing a planner can do changes that -- margin does not
  help, because the blow that missed dealt nothing at all.

  miss chance p |  1 blow  2 blows  3 blows  4 blows
          0.050 |  0.9500   0.9025   0.8574   0.8145 
          0.100 |  0.9000   0.8100   0.7290   0.6561 
          0.200 |  0.8000   0.6400   0.5120   0.4096 
          0.250 |  0.7500   0.5625   0.4219   0.3164 
          0.293 |  0.7070   0.4998   0.3534   0.2498    <-- two blows fall to a coin toss
          0.300 |  0.7000   0.4900   0.3430   0.2401 
          0.350 |  0.6500   0.4225   0.2746   0.1785 
          0.400 |  0.6000   0.3600   0.2160   0.1296 
          0.450 |  0.5500   0.3025   0.1664   0.0915 
          0.500 |  0.5000   0.2500   0.1250   0.0625 

  A two-move combination fails more often than it succeeds as soon as
  p > 1 - 1/sqrt(2) = 0.2929. A d20 attack roll needing 8 or more --
  a generous target -- misses 35% of the time, so the two-move
  combination the tactical layer exists to reward would land 42% of
  the time. That is this task's stop condition, and it is why option
  (a) was not taken.

  Option (c), both a to-hit roll and armour as reduction, carries the
  same (1-p)^n and adds the magnitude spread on top, so it is strictly
  worse for planning than either of the other two.

  Option (b) as shipped has no such term: every blow lands, so a plan
  can only be off by how much. Section 4b above is the price of that,
  and it is paid in points rather than in whole failed plans.
