Which space a chosen position is in: the same measurement either side of the
decision.

The command, which anyone can re-run:

    tools/position_space_probe.sh

It replays the shipped seeded model run -- ./run_agent.sh, seed 1234, 160 ticks
-- against whatever exchange is checked in at net/model_recording.gd, and prints
every turn on which a model named a place by its coordinates rather than by an
id. No key, no network, no model; two runs print identical bytes.

It measures the recording that is checked in, so the two captures below were
taken at two commits. The first was taken with the tree at 2c1b23b -- the
recording of 2026-09-03, against a menu on which `go_to` took `target` and
nothing else. The second was taken after `go_to` grew its second shape and the
exchange was re-recorded live on 2026-09-04. To re-take the first:

    git stash push -- $(git diff --name-only)   # or: git checkout 2c1b23b
    tools/position_space_probe.sh
    git stash pop

Three ways were open and the second was taken.

  1. The ground window could carry a cell's world coordinate as well as its
     height. Refused: the packet already tells the looker where it is standing,
     in world coordinates, on the line above the window -- the model had that
     number every time and still wrote a small one. An anchor is not the gap.
     The gap is that nothing says which space the *parameter* is in, and a
     packet cannot say that without holding a sentence about what an action
     means, which it is not allowed to hold.
  2. `go_to` grows a relative form on the action surface. Taken. Section 10's
     own call surface spells the row four ways and one of them is
     `MoveRelative(offset)`; the row has carried that spelling since the two
     lists were made one, and offered no parameter it could be written with. So
     this is finishing a row that already claimed the shape rather than
     inventing one, and it puts the coordinate space in the name of a key on the
     action surface -- where what an action means belongs -- instead of in a
     sentence in the packet, where it is forbidden.
  3. Nothing changes and the engine's refusal is the whole answer. Refused on
     the numbers below: it was every position the model named, not some of them.

What changed, and nothing else did: `go_to` grew an `offset` key beside its
`target` key, exactly one of the two is given, the menu prints one line per
shape, and the exchange was re-recorded because the prompt had changed. The
observation packet was not touched: not one word of `sim/observation.gd` moved.

The short of it. Before: 3 of 81 turns named a place, every one of them under
`target`, every one of them refused, and every one of them a walk of six hundred
units that would have been thirteen had the same two numbers meant an offset --
all three were the prompt's own example value, (12.5, -4.0), copied. After: 13
of 69 turns named a place, every one of them under `offset`, none refused for
being out of reach; the engine walked five of them, refused four because the
character was in a fight (which is not about coordinates at all) and four were
still running when the run ended.

One thing measured in between and thrown away. The first menu wrote the two
shapes on one line joined by a bar -- `target=#7 or (12.5, -4.0) | offset=(+2.0,
-6.0)` -- and the model copied the whole line: 5 of the 15 walks in that
recording carried both keys, `go_to target=#2 offset=(-2.0, -6.0)`, and the
catalogue refused every one of them. A line carrying both shapes is a line that
gets copied whole, so the menu now prints one line per shape and that recording
was replaced.
