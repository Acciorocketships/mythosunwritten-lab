The walk ends in the same place either way, and the engine's own answer is
word-for-word the same:

  before   t= 21  Pip    finished go_to(target=(17.566, 3.927)) -> go_to ok at=(17.566, 3.927) walked=18.0 steps=20

Eighteen units at 0.9 a stride is twenty strides, and a `go_to` costs twenty
ticks, so the span takes every stride the walk needed and the resolution
finishes nothing. A walk aimed further than its span reaches -- Odo's 42-unit
leg in the five-character scenario -- takes twenty strides in the span and the
remaining twenty-seven in the resolution, and lands in the same place it always
did.

Before, the character stands still 95.3% of the time and the four ticks it does
move are read as a run or a hop, because eighteen units in one tick is not a
walk by any threshold. After, it walks 98.5% of the time at 0.900 units a tick,
which is where `CharacterView.WALK_SPEED` (0.05) and `RUN_SPEED` (1.30) were
placed. Nothing in the render layer was tuned to make this happen; the
thresholds are the ones that were already there.

4. determinism
==============================================================================

  ./run_headless.sh --seed 1234 --ticks 200 > a.txt
  ./run_headless.sh --seed 1234 --ticks 200 > b.txt
  cmp a.txt b.txt

Two processes, one seed, 200 ticks: byte-identical.

  sha256  cc8bb4bfb82a800ac73b3795f822491eda07781f8a5d26def0cf59e5814cd7bb  (both)
  final fingerprint  efab1f61f1c6689f

Where a walk ends is unchanged by construction rather than by measurement: the
strides taken during the span and the strides taken by the resolution are the
same `Walk.stride` calls in the same order, so `tests/test_walk_motion.gd`
compares the spread walk against the same walk resolved in one call and requires
the coordinates to be equal exactly, not within a tolerance.

The seed-1234 100-tick world fingerprint moved:

  before  5014980a58150055
  after   baac1b9efedf472a

It moved because the cast is somewhere different on every tick of a walk, so the
ground streamed around it, the islands loaded and the props grown all differ
from tick to tick. It is a different world state at tick 100, reached the same
way twice.

5. a walk interrupted part-way
==============================================================================

From reports/scenario-evidence.txt, seed 1234. Wren sets off for the pile (#6)
at tick 6 and is spoken to at tick 11, five ticks into a twenty-tick walk:

  t=  6  Wren   began go_to(target=6), 20 ticks
  t= 11  Wren   interrupted (spoken to), abandoned go_to(target=6) 5/20t
  t= 11  Wren   began go_to(target=6), 20 ticks
  t= 31  Wren   finished go_to(target=6) -> go_to ok at=(-473.400, 416.000) walked=8.1 steps=9

Before, the second walk covered 12.6 units in 14 strides, because the first had
moved Wren nowhere. After, it covers 8.1 in 9, because the first walk had
already carried her 4.5 units -- five strides of 0.9 -- toward the pile. The two
add to 12.6 either way, and she arrives at the same (-473.400, 416.000).

The abandoned walk still never reaches the engine: `ControlLoop.actions_of`
counts it as nothing, and the suite checks that. What changed is that giving up
on a walk half way now leaves the character half way, which is what happened.

==============================================================================
6. one movement implementation, found by scanning
==============================================================================

`tests/test_walk_motion.gd` reads every `.gd` file under sim/, render/, bin/,
net/, tools/ and tests/ and collects every line that *adds to* a combatant's
ground coordinates. Advancing a position adds; putting somebody down at one
sets, and every other place in the project that touches those fields sets them.
The scan finds four lines in two files and the suite requires exactly that:

  res://sim/combatant.gd:145 x += cos(heading) * speed     the drift along a heading
  res://sim/combatant.gd:146 z += sin(heading) * speed
  res://sim/walk.gd:131 actor.x += step.x                  the stride toward a place
  res://sim/walk.gd:132 actor.z += step.y

`ActionEngine._go_to` holds no step arithmetic of its own any more: it aims a
`Walk` and turns `Walk.stride` until it stops returning true. `ControlLoop`
calls the same `Walk.stride`, once per tick. The scan is then run over a planted
line that would break the claim, and must catch it.

The scan also reaches the suites, where it finds six lines shifting a fixture
along to set a case up (test_water, test_observation, test_combat_snap,
test_player_actions). Those are named separately rather than hidden.

8. what this breaks, measured, and not fixed here
==============================================================================

The checked-in model recording no longer answers the questions the shipped model
run puts. Measured, replaying `./run_agent.sh` against `net/model_recording.gd`:

                                              before    after
  recorded rows                                   79       79
  questions the run puts                          71       71
  questions with a reply recorded for them        71       15
  questions with none                              0       56
  recorded replies never claimed                   8       64

`ModelChannel` matches a recorded reply to a question by the fingerprint of the
prompt and falls back to position when no unspent row fingerprints the same. The
prompt carries the character's own position to three decimal places and its
heading, so a world in which a character stands still for nineteen ticks and
then jumps produces one set of prompts and a world in which it walks produces
another. That is what moved: the recording was replayable because the world was
not moving.

The visible consequence is one failing check in the whole suite --
`1 of 51 suites failed (1 failed check of 196501)`:

  FAIL  agent  1124 checks, 1 failed
    - the run ran out of recorded replies: Odo: this reply was recorded for
      another question: the prompt put here fingerprints 69bc6ed7467c7af4 and
      the recorded one bfc8438137a15413

The documented fix is `OPENROUTER_API_KEY=... ./run_record.sh --live`, which is
the only command in the repository that makes a network call. It replaces the
shipped exchange, and every report that quotes that exchange -- its date, its
model, its reply count and the replies themselves -- moves with it. That is a
call to make deliberately rather than as a side effect of a movement fix, so it
is reported here rather than taken.

Everything else in the suite passes: 50 of 51 suites, including the new `walk
motion` suite (93 checks) and `control loop` (78). `./run_tests.sh --layers-only`
passes.
